Does the folder earn its place?

A folder of instructions is meant to help an AI answer questions about a set of documents. This test checked whether it does.

No. The folder got the AI to its answers in half the steps, but its answers were marked right less often than with the documents alone.

The experiment

The same 10 questions, asked of the same AI, in three set-ups. Each set-up was asked every question 3 times.

The design

What makes it an experiment and not a demonstration.

The one thing changedWhether the folder's 4 instruction files are loaded before the question.
Kept the sameThe AI model and its settings, the 18 documents, the 10 questions, the tools (search, list, open), and a fresh session for every question.
What was measuredSteps taken, answers marked right, text put in front of the AI, tokens and seconds.
The controlsNothing at all. The documents alone. The folder present but not loaded. A folder with its map made wrong on purpose.
The sample10 questions, asked 3 times each, in 19 set-ups: 570 sessions. Then 10 more questions held back as a check.
The decision ruleA difference counts only if it shows on 9 or 10 of the 10 questions. Written down before any document existed.
Keeping bias outAnother AI wrote the questions and never saw the folder. A third AI checked the answers. The exam was drawn at random. The order of sessions was random. Graders were not told which set-up wrote an answer.
Keeping it honestThe sentences that could be published were written first. Every session is kept. The results are built from the raw logs by a script that anyone can run again.

How it was run

Each step was committed in this order. The rule, the questions and the folder were all fixed before the run.

  1. Rule fixedHow the folder would be judged, written before anything else.
  2. DocumentsChatGPT wrote 18 documents for two made-up organisations.
  3. QuestionsChatGPT wrote 20, with answers. It never saw the folder.
  4. Answers checkedGemini answered all 20 with no answer key. No figure differed.
  5. Random drawA script picked the 10 exam questions. Nobody chose.
  6. 570 sessionsClaude answered in 19 set-ups, from one command.
  7. Graded 3 timesTwice by Claude, once by Gemini, set-up hidden.
  8. Results builtBy script, from the raw logs. All of it is in the repo.

Why the folder lost marks

The folder tells the AI to answer what was asked and then stop. Its answers were right on the main point and less than half the length. The answer key also wanted the detail a longer answer includes, such as how far over a limit something was.

What the control changed

A follow-up, after the verdict. One line was added to the folder's rules: "Say what would otherwise apply, and the difference." Then the three set-ups were run again, on the 10 exam questions and on the 10 kept aside.

The lost marks came back30 of 30 on all three gradings, on both sets of questions. It was 23 to 28.
The steps did not move2.33 against 5.20. Fewer on 10 of 10.
The text is still more9,579 against 7,665. By the same rule, still not earned.

The line was written after seeing which answers lost marks, so it is fitted to those questions. The first verdict stands.

What held up
  • The map does the work on steps: 2.50 with the map alone, 6.07 with nothing loaded.
  • A re-run on a second computer, set up from nothing, gave the same result: 2.30 against 5.20.
  • On 10 questions kept aside: fewer steps on all 10, and 30 of 30 marked right in both set-ups. The loss of marks did not repeat.
What this does not show
  • One AI model, made-up documents, ten questions, one day.
  • The graders could tell the two set-ups apart from the writing.
  • The second computer used the same account. Nobody outside has run it yet.
