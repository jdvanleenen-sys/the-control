# Q3, run 2

## Prompt, exactly as sent

> Under the Fenwray Film Society rules: At a screening on 14 November 2025, a visitor makes a serious threat against another attendee. The visitor has not previously been warned about their behaviour. Must the duty volunteer issue a warning before requiring the visitor to leave?

4. Read reference/film/film-changes.md

## Answer

**No.** A warning isn't required first. Section 2.3 of the Conduct and Complaints rules (`reference\film\film-conduct.md:22`) says "Serious threats may result in immediate removal without a warning."

- **Rules in force:** The conduct rules were adopted on 18 March 2025 and took effect on 1 April 2025, so they applied on 14 November 2025. The changes register has no amendment to the conduct rules.
- **Threats are prohibited:** Section 1.2 prohibits threats. Section 1.3 requires visitors to follow reasonable instructions from duty volunteers.
- **Removal is discretionary:** Section 2.3 says "may", so the volunteer can remove the visitor immediately but doesn't have to. The usual route in sections 2.1 and 2.2 is to ask the person to stop and require them to leave only if they refuse. A serious threat lets the volunteer skip that step.
- **Record the incident:** The duty volunteer must record it within 48 hours (section 2.4).
- **Other effects:** Removal doesn't end the visitor's society membership (section 2.5), and it doesn't automatically give them a refund right (section 5.3).
