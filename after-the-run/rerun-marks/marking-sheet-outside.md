## Answer 9387

**Question:** Under the Quillbrook Village Hall rules: An organiser plans a children's event in the Meeting Room on 19 October 2025. There will be 16 children aged 8 to 11 and five adults, including organisers. The organiser wants to know whether the attendance is permitted and whether the children may be left without adult supervision while the adults prepare another room. What do the rules require?

**Expected answer:** The attendance is permitted because the Meeting Room capacity is 24 people and the total is 21, including the adults and children. However, children under 12 must remain under adult supervision. The organiser therefore cannot leave the children unsupervised while the adults prepare another room.

**Correct requires:** The answer must give a total attendance of 21 against the capacity of 24, permit the attendance and require continuing adult supervision. It is wrong if it rejects the event by counting children and adults against separate capacity allowances, counts only the five adults, or says that being below capacity permits the 16 children to remain unsupervised.

**The answer to mark:**

**Attendance: permitted.**
- The Meeting Room holds a maximum of 24 people (hall-bookings.md §4.2).
- Capacity counts organisers and children (§4.4). That makes 16 children + 5 adults = 21, which is 3 under the limit.
- The fire safety rules in hall-safety.md also apply (§4.5). The hirer must count attendees when the event begins (hall-safety.md §3.2).
- 19 October 2025 is a Sunday, so the event must finish and everyone must leave by 21:00 (hall-bookings.md §3.3–3.4). Setup and clearing must also fall within booked hours (§3.5).

**Leaving the children unsupervised: not permitted.**
- Children under 12 must remain under adult supervision (hall-safety.md §3.3). All the children are 8 to 11, so the rule covers every one of them.
- The adults can still prepare the other room, but at least one adult must stay with the children.
- At least one responsible adult must also remain near the main entrance (§3.4). The rules don't say whether the same adult can cover both duties. It is safer to have the supervising adult in a position that also covers the entrance, or to leave a second adult there.
- The hirer must remain on site throughout the event (hall-conduct.md §1.3) and is responsible for guests (§1.2).
- Corridors and access routes must stay clear (hall-safety.md §3.5), so don't stage materials for the other room in them.

Nothing in the committee changes register (hall-changes.md) alters any of these provisions for this date.

## Answer 2502

**Question:** Under the Fenwray Film Society rules: At a screening on 14 November 2025, a visitor makes a serious threat against another attendee. The visitor has not previously been warned about their behaviour. Must the duty volunteer issue a warning before requiring the visitor to leave?

**Expected answer:** No. Serious threats can result in immediate removal without a warning. Although the ordinary disruption procedure allows a volunteer to ask someone to stop and then require them to leave if they refuse, the serious-threat exception permits immediate removal.

**Correct requires:** The answer must say that no prior warning is required for a serious threat and distinguish that exception from the ordinary stop-and-refuse procedure. It is wrong if it insists that the visitor must first receive a warning or refuse an instruction before removal is permitted.

**The answer to mark:**

**No. A warning isn't required.** Under section 2.3 of the Fenwray Film Society conduct rules (`film-conduct.md`), "Serious threats may result in immediate removal without a warning." That covers a visitor who makes a serious threat at the 14 November 2025 screening, even if they had no earlier warning.

- **Rules in force:** The conduct rules were adopted on 18 March 2025 and took effect on 1 April 2025, so they applied on that date. The changes register has no amendment to the conduct rules.
- **Threats are prohibited:** Section 1.2 prohibits threats, and section 1.3 requires visitors to follow reasonable instructions from duty volunteers. Section 2.2 lets a volunteer require someone who refuses to stop disruptive behaviour to leave. Section 2.3 is the route for serious threats, because it skips the ask-to-stop step in section 2.1.
- **"May", not "must":** Section 2.3 gives the volunteer discretion. They can remove the visitor immediately, or they can still choose to warn first.
- **Record the incident:** The duty volunteer must record it within 48 hours (section 2.4).
- **Refunds:** Removal doesn't automatically give the visitor a refund right (section 5.3). Eligibility is decided under `film-refunds.md`.
