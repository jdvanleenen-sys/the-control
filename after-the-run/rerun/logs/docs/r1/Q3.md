# Q3, run 1

## Prompt, exactly as sent

> Under the Fenwray Film Society rules: At a screening on 14 November 2025, a visitor makes a serious threat against another attendee. The visitor has not previously been warned about their behaviour. Must the duty volunteer issue a warning before requiring the visitor to leave?

3. Read reference/film/film-conduct.md
4. Read reference/film/film-changes.md

## Answer

**No.** The duty volunteer doesn't have to warn the visitor first.

Section 2.3 of the Conduct and Complaints rules (`film-conduct.md`) says "Serious threats may result in immediate removal without a warning." Those rules were adopted on 18 March 2025 and took effect on 1 April 2025, so they applied on 14 November 2025. The committee's changes register doesn't amend them.

- **Removal is discretionary.** The rule says "may", so the volunteer can remove the visitor immediately but doesn't have to. Section 2.2 also lets a volunteer require a person who refuses to stop disruptive behaviour to leave. A serious threat falls under the prohibition on threats in 1.2, and under 1.3 the visitor must follow reasonable instructions to leave.
- **Record the incident.** The volunteer must record it within 48 hours (2.4). For a 14 November screening, that is by 16 November.
- **Membership is unaffected.** Immediate removal doesn't itself end membership (2.5). Any suspension would go through the complaints and suspension process in sections 3 and 4.
- **No automatic refund.** Being removed doesn't create a refund right (5.3). Eligibility is decided under `film-refunds.md` (5.4).
