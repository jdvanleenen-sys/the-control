## Prompt, exactly as sent

> Under the Quillbrook Village Hall rules: Four trustees want to attend a committee meeting on 15 November 2026, but one proposes joining by video call from home while the other three attend in person. Do the rules allow a remote trustee to count towards the required quorum, and would that make this meeting quorate?

## Answer

**The rules don't say, so I can't confirm the meeting would be quorate.**

- **Quorum:** Rule 4.2 in `hall-trustees.md` says "Four trustees form a quorum." It doesn't say how a trustee counts as present, for example whether they must be in the room.
- **Remote attendance:** Rule 6.3 says the rules "do not specify whether trustees may attend remotely." So nothing allows a video-call trustee to count towards quorum, and nothing rules it out.
- **Other documents:** The changes register (`hall-changes.md`) has no entry on this. A search of the other hall documents found no mention of remote, video or online attendance.

**Your meeting on 15 November 2026:** With three trustees in the room and one on video, quorum depends on whether the remote trustee counts as present. The rules leave that open.

- If the video trustee counts, there are four and the meeting is quorate.
- If only in-person attendance counts, there are three and the meeting is not quorate. Any decisions taken could then be open to challenge.

**Safer options:**
1. Get a fourth trustee to attend in person. This avoids the question.
2. Before the meeting, have the committee adopt a rule or resolution that allows remote attendance and counts it towards quorum. Any change would need a vote under rule 4.3.
3. If neither is possible, treat the meeting as informal and confirm any decisions at a later quorate meeting.

I'd go with option 1 or 2 rather than rely on the rules being silent.
