## Prompt, exactly as sent

> Under the Fenwray Film Society rules: At a screening on 14 November 2025, a visitor makes a serious threat against another attendee. The visitor has not previously been warned about their behaviour. Must the duty volunteer issue a warning before requiring the visitor to leave?

## Answer

**No. A warning isn't required.** Under section 2.3 of the Fenwray Film Society conduct rules (`film-conduct.md`), "Serious threats may result in immediate removal without a warning." That covers a visitor who makes a serious threat at the 14 November 2025 screening, even if they had no earlier warning.

- **Rules in force:** The conduct rules were adopted on 18 March 2025 and took effect on 1 April 2025, so they applied on that date. The changes register has no amendment to the conduct rules.
- **Threats are prohibited:** Section 1.2 prohibits threats, and section 1.3 requires visitors to follow reasonable instructions from duty volunteers. Section 2.2 lets a volunteer require someone who refuses to stop disruptive behaviour to leave. Section 2.3 is the route for serious threats, because it skips the ask-to-stop step in section 2.1.
- **"May", not "must":** Section 2.3 gives the volunteer discretion. They can remove the visitor immediately, or they can still choose to warn first.
- **Record the incident:** The duty volunteer must record it within 48 hours (section 2.4).
- **Refunds:** Removal doesn't automatically give the visitor a refund right (section 5.3). Eligibility is decided under `film-refunds.md`.
