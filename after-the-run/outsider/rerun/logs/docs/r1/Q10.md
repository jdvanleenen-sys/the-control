# Q10, run 1

## Prompt, exactly as sent

> Under the Quillbrook Village Hall rules: Four trustees want to attend a committee meeting on 15 November 2026, but one proposes joining by video call from home while the other three attend in person. Do the rules allow a remote trustee to count towards the required quorum, and would that make this meeting quorate?

## Answer

**The rules don't say whether a remote trustee counts towards the quorum, so they don't settle whether this meeting is quorate.**

- **Quorum:** Rule 4.2 says "Four trustees form a quorum." With four trustees wanting to attend, the meeting reaches quorum only if all four count.
- **Remote attendance:** Rule 6.3 says the rules "do not specify whether trustees may attend remotely." Nothing in the rules either allows or bars a video-call attendee from counting. Rule 4.2 doesn't say the four must be physically present, and nothing else in the hall rules addresses it.

**Consequences for 15 November 2026:**
- If the video attendee counts, there are four trustees and the meeting is quorate.
- If only in-person attendees count, there are three, the meeting is inquorate, and any decisions made could be open to challenge.

**Options:**
1. Get the remote trustee to attend in person, which removes the doubt.
2. Have the committee agree beforehand, and minute it, that remote attendance counts. Do this only if the committee has the authority to decide that. The rules don't say who does.
3. Add a fifth trustee in person, so the meeting is quorate either way.
4. Amend the rules to cover remote attendance. Check whether the rules say how they can be changed.

I only checked the hall rules in `reference\hall`. If the hall has a constitution, a governing document or legal advice that covers remote attendance, that could change the answer.
