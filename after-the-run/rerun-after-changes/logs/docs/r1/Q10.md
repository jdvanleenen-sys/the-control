## Prompt, exactly as sent

> Under the Quillbrook Village Hall rules: Four trustees want to attend a committee meeting on 15 November 2026, but one proposes joining by video call from home while the other three attend in person. Do the rules allow a remote trustee to count towards the required quorum, and would that make this meeting quorate?

## Answer

**The rules don't say whether a remote trustee counts, so they don't settle whether the meeting is quorate.**

- **Quorum:** Rule 4.2 says "Four trustees form a quorum." Four trustees are planning to attend, so the number is enough if all four count.
- **Remote attendance:** Rule 6.3 says the rules "do not specify whether trustees may attend remotely." Nothing in them allows a video-call attendee to count towards the quorum, and nothing rules it out. Rule 4.2 doesn't say the four must be physically present, but it doesn't say they can attend remotely either.

With only the three in-person trustees counted, the meeting would be one short of quorum. If the remote trustee counts, it would be quorate.

Because the rules are silent, the committee would need to resolve this itself before 15 November 2026. It could:
- Agree a rule change or a formal resolution allowing remote attendance to count. I'd check how the rules say they can be amended, though the file I read doesn't cover that.
- Check whether the hall has a separate constitution or governing document that addresses remote attendance.
- Ask the fourth trustee to attend in person, or bring in a fifth trustee, so the meeting is quorate either way.

The last option is the safest, because decisions made at an inquorate meeting could be challenged.
