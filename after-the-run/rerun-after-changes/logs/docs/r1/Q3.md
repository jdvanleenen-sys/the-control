## Prompt, exactly as sent

> Under the Fenwray Film Society rules: At a screening on 14 November 2025, a visitor makes a serious threat against another attendee. The visitor has not previously been warned about their behaviour. Must the duty volunteer issue a warning before requiring the visitor to leave?

## Answer

**No.** Under the Fenwray Film Society conduct rules, the duty volunteer doesn't have to warn the visitor first.

- **Section 2.3 of `film-conduct.md`:** "Serious threats may result in immediate removal without a warning." A serious threat against another attendee falls within this. The visitor's lack of any earlier warning doesn't change that.
- **Section 2.2:** Normally a person is required to leave only after they refuse to stop disruptive behaviour (section 2.1). Section 2.3 is an exception to that step-by-step approach for serious threats.
- **Section 1.2:** Threats are prohibited outright, and section 1.3 requires visitors to follow reasonable instructions from duty volunteers.

The rule says "may", so removal without a warning is allowed but not compulsory. The volunteer can choose to give a warning first.

If the volunteer removes the visitor, they must record the incident within 48 hours (section 2.4). Removal doesn't automatically give the visitor a refund right (section 5.3).

The conduct rules were adopted on 18 March 2025 and took effect on 1 April 2025, so they applied on 14 November 2025. The changes register has no amendment to the conduct rules.
