## Prompt, exactly as sent

> Under the Fenwray Film Society rules: At a screening on 14 November 2025, a visitor makes a serious threat against another attendee. The visitor has not previously been warned about their behaviour. Must the duty volunteer issue a warning before requiring the visitor to leave?

## Answer

**No. The duty volunteer doesn't have to warn the visitor first.**

Under section 2.3 of the Conduct and Complaints rules (`film-conduct.md`), "Serious threats may result in immediate removal without a warning." Section 1.2 prohibits threats outright. The rules were effective from 1 April 2025, so they applied on 14 November 2025. The committee's changes register has no amendment to the conduct rules.

- **Warning route:** The warning-first process in sections 2.1 and 2.2 applies to ordinary disruptive behaviour. There, the volunteer asks the person to stop, and only if they refuse can they be required to leave. A serious threat bypasses that step.
- **Discretion:** Section 2.3 says removal "may" happen immediately. So removal without a warning is allowed, not mandatory. The volunteer could still choose to warn first.
- **Recording:** The volunteer must record the incident within 48 hours (section 2.4).
- **Membership and refunds:** Removal doesn't itself end membership (section 2.5), and it doesn't automatically create a refund right (section 5.3).
