> Under the Fenwray Film Society rules: At a screening on 14 November 2025, a visitor makes a serious threat against another attendee. The visitor has not previously been warned about their behaviour. Must the duty volunteer issue a warning before requiring the visitor to leave?

## Answer

**No.** Under section 2.3 of the Conduct and Complaints rules (`film-conduct.md`), "Serious threats may result in immediate removal without a warning." The duty volunteer can therefore require the visitor to leave straight away, even though the visitor has had no earlier warning.

- **Why section 2.3 applies:** Section 1.2 prohibits threats, and section 1.3 requires visitors to follow reasonable instructions from duty volunteers. The rules were adopted on 18 March 2025 and took effect on 1 April 2025, so they were in force on 14 November 2025.
- **No amendments:** The committee changes register (`film-changes.md`) has no amendment to the conduct rules.
- **Warning is optional:** The warn-then-require-to-leave steps in sections 2.1 and 2.2 cover ordinary disruption. For a serious threat, the volunteer can skip the warning.
- **Still required:** The volunteer must record the incident within 48 hours (section 2.4).
- **Other effects:** Removal doesn't end the visitor's society membership (section 2.5). It also doesn't automatically give them a refund right (section 5.3).

Section 2.3 says removal "may" happen without a warning, so the volunteer has discretion. The rules don't define "serious", so that judgement rests with the volunteer.
