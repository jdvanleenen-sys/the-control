**Question:** Under the Fenwray Film Society rules: A parent brings their 9-year-old child to a public screening on 16 August 2025 and accompanies them throughout. A volunteer has found an old price list showing that children under 12 pay £2. What is the correct ticket price for the child?

**Expected answer:** The correct ticket price is £3. The £2 price appears in the archived schedule that was superseded on 1 March 2025. The governing schedule charges accompanied children under 12 £3, and no later change alters that concession.

**Correct requires:** The answer must give £3, noting that the child is under 12 and accompanied by an adult. It is wrong if it gives £2 from the archived schedule, £5 for the 12–15 age group, or £7 for standard admission while overlooking the applicable child concession.

**The answer to mark:**

**The child's ticket is £3.**

- **Current rule:** Section 2.3 of `film-fees.md` (effective 1 March 2025) says children under 12 pay £3 when accompanied by an adult. The child is 9 and the parent is with them throughout, so this applies.
- **The £2 price list is out of date:** £2 comes from section 2.3 of `film-fees-archive.md`. That schedule applied only from 1 to 28 February 2025 and was superseded in full on 1 March 2025. The archive says it "must not be used for current charges."
- **No later change affects it:** The committee changes register (`film-changes.md`) only changes the member admission price, adult membership, equipment, capacity and refund notice. None of those touch the under-12 price, so £3 still applies on 16 August 2025.

## Answer 6755

**Question:** Under the Fenwray Film Society rules: At a screening on 14 November 2025, a visitor makes a serious threat against another attendee. The visitor has not previously been warned about their behaviour. Must the duty volunteer issue a warning before requiring the visitor to leave?

**Expected answer:** No. Serious threats can result in immediate removal without a warning. Although the ordinary disruption procedure allows a volunteer to ask someone to stop and then require them to leave if they refuse, the serious-threat exception permits immediate removal.

**Correct requires:** The answer must say that no prior warning is required for a serious threat and distinguish that exception from the ordinary stop-and-refuse procedure. It is wrong if it insists that the visitor must first receive a warning or refuse an instruction before removal is permitted.

**The answer to mark:**

**No. The duty volunteer doesn't have to warn the visitor first.**

The rules were effective from 1 April 2025, so they applied on 14 November 2025. The committee's changes register has no amendment to the conduct rules.

- **Warning route:** The warning-first process in sections 2.1 and 2.2 applies to ordinary disruptive behaviour. There, the volunteer asks the person to stop, and only if they refuse can they be required to leave. A serious threat bypasses that step.
- **Discretion:** Section 2.3 says removal "may" happen immediately. So removal without a warning is allowed, not mandatory. The volunteer could still choose to warn first.
- **Recording:** The volunteer must record the incident within 48 hours (section 2.4).
- **Membership and refunds:** Removal doesn't itself end membership (section 2.5), and it doesn't automatically create a refund right (section 5.3).

**Expected answer:** The total equipment charge is £25. The portable speaker costs £10 per booking and folding display boards cost £5 per set per booking. Three sets cost £15, so the speaker and boards together cost £10 + £15 = £25.

**Correct requires:** The answer must give £25 and show £10 + (3 × £5). It is wrong if it charges £15 by treating all three sets of boards as one set, £13 by using the archived speaker and board prices as one-time charges, or £20 by charging for only two sets.

**The answer to mark:**

The total equipment charge is **£25**.

- Portable speaker: 1 × £10 = £10 (hall-fees.md §4.2)
- Folding display boards: 3 sets × £5 = £15 (hall-fees.md §4.3)

The current fee schedule applies because the booking starts after 1 April 2025 (§1.2). The older archive rates (£8 for the speaker, £4 per set of boards) don't apply. None of the committee changes touch equipment charges, and the group discount doesn't apply to equipment anyway (§3.4).

The Meeting Room is booked, so the equipment can be hired with it (§4.4). The hall must still confirm the equipment is available, and the hirer must request it when booking (hall-bookings.md §5.3).
