**Question:** Under the Fenwray Film Society rules: A member aged 35 has an active annual membership but is suspended by the committee from 1 November through 20 November 2025. They arrive at a public screening on 8 November 2025 and ask to pay the usual member admission price. Are they entitled to the discount, and what admission price applies instead?

**Expected answer:** No, the suspended member is not entitled to the member discount during the suspension. Suspended members cannot claim member prices, even when their annual membership would otherwise be active. The standard admission price on 8 November 2025 is £7 rather than the £4 active-member price.

**Correct requires:** The answer must deny the discount and give £7 as the applicable standard admission charge, contrasting it with the £4 member price. It is wrong if it charges £4 simply because the annual membership has not expired, £5 by confusing the child ticket band or later member rate, or says suspension permanently ends the membership.

**The answer to mark:**

**No, they aren't entitled to the member discount. They pay the standard admission price of £7.**

- **Suspension:** Under `film-membership.md` §5.4, a suspended member "cannot vote, book equipment or claim member prices." Having an active annual membership doesn't change that. The suspension runs 1–20 November 2025, which covers the screening on 8 November. It is also within the 30-day maximum in `film-conduct.md` §4.1. `film-conduct.md` §4.4 sends the question of rights during suspension back to the membership rules.
- **Price:** `film-fees.md` §2.1 sets standard admission at £7 per screening. The member price in §2.2 was £4. A committee decision of 19 January 2026 raised it to £5, but only from 1 February 2026. That change doesn't apply to an 8 November 2025 screening, so it doesn't matter here.
- **Age:** At 35, the member isn't in either child category (§2.3, §2.4), so the standard rate applies.

I'm assuming the suspension was validly imposed under `film-conduct.md` §4.2–4.3, with a majority vote and a written decision stating the reason and dates. If it wasn't, the outcome could differ.
