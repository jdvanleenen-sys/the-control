## Prompt, exactly as sent

> Under the Quillbrook Village Hall rules: Four trustees want to attend a committee meeting on 15 November 2026, but one proposes joining by video call from home while the other three attend in person. Do the rules allow a remote trustee to count towards the required quorum, and would that make this meeting quorate?

## Answer

**The rules don't say whether a remote trustee counts towards the quorum, so they don't settle whether the meeting is quorate.**

- **Quorum:** Rule 4.2 says "Four trustees form a quorum." It doesn't say they must be physically present.
- **Remote attendance:** Rule 6.3 says the rules "do not specify whether trustees may attend remotely." That is an acknowledged gap, not a permission or a ban.

If the four trustees (three in person, one by video) were counted, the meeting would reach the quorum of four. If the remote trustee isn't counted, only three are present and the meeting would not be quorate. Nothing in the rules decides between the two.

Because a meeting that isn't quorate may not be able to take valid decisions, I wouldn't rely on the remote trustee to make up the numbers. The safest options are:
1. Get a fourth trustee to attend in person.
2. Have the committee agree in advance, at an earlier quorate meeting, how remote attendance is treated, and minute that decision. Rule 4.5 requires minutes to be approved at the next meeting.

The hall's rules also don't say who decides on a gap like this. If you have a constitution or governing document beyond these rules, check it as well.
