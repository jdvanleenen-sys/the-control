# Q3, run 2

## Prompt, exactly as sent

> Under the Fenwray Film Society rules: At a screening on 14 November 2025, a visitor makes a serious threat against another attendee. The visitor has not previously been warned about their behaviour. Must the duty volunteer issue a warning before requiring the visitor to leave?

## Answer

**No.** Under the Fenwray Film Society conduct rules, the duty volunteer doesn't have to warn the visitor first.

- **Section 2.3 of `film-conduct.md`:** "Serious threats may result in immediate removal without a warning." The rule applies to a serious threat against another attendee, and it lets the volunteer remove the visitor straight away.
- **Sections 2.1 and 2.2:** Asking someone to stop and then requiring them to leave if they refuse is the usual sequence. It applies to ordinary disruptive behaviour. Section 2.3 is an exception for serious threats, so the visitor's lack of a previous warning doesn't matter.
- **Rule 1.2:** Threats are prohibited, and rule 1.3 requires visitors to follow reasonable instructions from duty volunteers.
- **Date:** The conduct rules were effective from 1 April 2025, so they cover a screening on 14 November 2025. The changes register (`film-changes.md`) doesn't amend them.

Some related points:
- The volunteer must record the incident within 48 hours (2.4).
- Removal doesn't end membership (2.5). It also doesn't create a right to a refund (5.3). `film-refunds.md` decides refund eligibility (5.4).
- The wording is "may result in immediate removal", so removal is allowed but not compulsory. The volunteer has discretion.
