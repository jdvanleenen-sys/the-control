## Prompt, exactly as sent

> Under the Quillbrook Village Hall rules: Four trustees want to attend a committee meeting on 15 November 2026, but one proposes joining by video call from home while the other three attend in person. Do the rules allow a remote trustee to count towards the required quorum, and would that make this meeting quorate?

## Answer

**The rules don't say, so I can't confirm the meeting would be quorate.**

- **Quorum:** Rule 4.2 says "Four trustees form a quorum." There are four trustees in total, so all four would have to count.
- **Remote attendance:** Rule 6.3 says the rules "do not specify whether trustees may attend remotely." Nothing in rule 4 says whether someone joining by video counts as present. I also searched the other hall documents and found nothing on remote attendance.

If the video-call trustee counts, the meeting has exactly four and is quorate. If they don't, only three are present and the meeting is inquorate. Any decisions made then would be open to challenge. The rules don't settle which applies.

Because the quorum is exactly four, the meeting would have no margin. If any one of the four dropped out, including through a lost connection, it would lose its quorum.

Before 15 November, I'd suggest either:
1. Getting the committee to agree, and minute, that remote attendance counts toward the quorum. That would be a new decision, because the rules are silent. It would be safer to do this at a meeting before the 15th.
2. Having all four trustees attend in person, which avoids the question.

The hall's governing document may also say something. I only had the rules in this folder.
