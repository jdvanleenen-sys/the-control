## Prompt, exactly as sent

> Under the Fenwray Film Society rules: At a screening on 14 November 2025, a visitor makes a serious threat against another attendee. The visitor has not previously been warned about their behaviour. Must the duty volunteer issue a warning before requiring the visitor to leave?

## Answer

**No. A warning isn't required for a serious threat.**

Section 2.3 of the Conduct and Complaints rules (`film-conduct.md`) says "Serious threats may result in immediate removal without a warning." Threats are prohibited under 1.2. The general route in 2.1 and 2.2 is to ask the person to stop, and only a person who refuses may be required to leave. Section 2.3 is the exception to that route. So the duty volunteer can require the visitor to leave straight away, even though the visitor was never warned.

- **Judgement:** The rule says "may", so removal is at the volunteer's discretion. It also depends on the threat being "serious".
- **Recording:** The volunteer must record the incident within 48 hours (2.4).
- **Membership and refunds:** Immediate removal doesn't end society membership (2.5). It doesn't automatically give the visitor a refund right either (5.3).
- **Date:** The committee's changes register (`film-changes.md`) has no amendment to the conduct rules. The rules were effective from 1 April 2025, so they applied on 14 November 2025.
