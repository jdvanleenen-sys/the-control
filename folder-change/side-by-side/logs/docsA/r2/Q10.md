## Prompt, exactly as sent

> Under the Quillbrook Village Hall rules: Four trustees want to attend a committee meeting on 15 November 2026, but one proposes joining by video call from home while the other three attend in person. Do the rules allow a remote trustee to count towards the required quorum, and would that make this meeting quorate?

## Answer

**The rules don't say, so I can't confirm the meeting would be quorate.**

- **Quorum:** Rule 4.2 says "Four trustees form a quorum." It doesn't say the four must be physically present.
- **Remote attendance:** Rule 6.3 says the rules "do not specify whether trustees may attend remotely." Nothing in them allows or bars a video-call trustee from counting towards quorum.

The outcome depends on that gap:
- If the remote trustee counts, all four are present and the meeting is quorate.
- If only trustees in the room count, there are three, which is one short, and the meeting would not be quorate.

Under these rules, three in person plus one on video isn't safely quorate. Any decisions made could be open to challenge.

Before 15 November, the committee could:
1. Have all four trustees attend in person.
2. Agree and minute a resolution allowing remote attendance to count. Check first whether the hall's governing document or its charity or legal framework already covers this. These rules don't, and they may not be the only source.
3. Add a rule on remote attendance to settle it for future meetings.

I only checked the hall rule files in this folder. A separate governing document could answer the question, but I haven't seen one.
