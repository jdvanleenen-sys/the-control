## Prompt, exactly as sent

> Under the Fenwray Film Society rules: At a screening on 14 November 2025, a visitor makes a serious threat against another attendee. The visitor has not previously been warned about their behaviour. Must the duty volunteer issue a warning before requiring the visitor to leave?

## Answer

**No. The duty volunteer doesn't have to warn the visitor first.** Section 2.3 of the Conduct and Complaints rules (`film-conduct.md`) says "Serious threats may result in immediate removal without a warning."

- **Threats are prohibited.** Section 1.2 bans threats, and section 1.3 requires visitors to follow reasonable instructions from duty volunteers.
- **The warning process doesn't apply here.** Under sections 2.1 and 2.2, a volunteer can ask someone to stop disruptive behaviour and require a person who refuses to leave. A serious threat falls under the separate rule in 2.3, which allows removal without that step. The visitor having no earlier warnings doesn't change this.
- **The rule is discretionary.** The word is "may", so the volunteer is allowed to remove the visitor immediately but isn't required to.
- **The rules don't define "serious".** The volunteer has to judge that. The threat you describe is already serious.
- **Two follow-up steps apply.** The volunteer must record the incident within 48 hours (2.4). Removal doesn't end any society membership (2.5) and doesn't create a right to a refund (5.3).

The changes register (`film-changes.md`) doesn't amend the conduct rules. The 14 November 2025 date therefore doesn't affect the answer, because the conduct rules took effect on 1 April 2025.
