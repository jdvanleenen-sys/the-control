## Prompt, exactly as sent

> Under the Quillbrook Village Hall rules: Four trustees want to attend a committee meeting on 15 November 2026, but one proposes joining by video call from home while the other three attend in person. Do the rules allow a remote trustee to count towards the required quorum, and would that make this meeting quorate?

## Answer

**The rules don't say, so I can't confirm the meeting would be quorate.**

- **Quorum:** Rule 4.2 of the Trustee Committee Rules (`hall-trustees.md`) says "Four trustees form a quorum." There are four trustees in total, so all four would have to count.
- **Remote attendance:** Rule 6.3 says the rules "do not specify whether trustees may attend remotely." Nothing in 4.2 or elsewhere says that a trustee on a video call is "present" for quorum. Nothing says they aren't. I also checked the other hall documents, and none of them covers remote attendance.

With three trustees in the room, the meeting is quorate only if the video-call trustee counts. The rules neither allow nor exclude that, so it's a gap the committee would have to settle.

Some practical options:
- **Safest:** get all four trustees into the room on 15 November.
- **Alternatively:** add a fifth trustee in person. The committee should have five to nine trustees under rule 2.1, so four may mean a vacancy. Under rule 3.3, the committee can fill vacancies.
- **If the remote trustee must join by video:** have the committee agree in advance, and record in the minutes, that remote attendees count as present. Better still, amend the rules to say so. Any decisions made at that meeting could be open to challenge if the quorum is later disputed.

If the hall has a constitution or governing document outside these files, it may cover remote attendance. I'd check that before relying on the video-call trustee.
