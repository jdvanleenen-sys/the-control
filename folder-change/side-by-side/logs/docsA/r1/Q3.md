## Prompt, exactly as sent

> Under the Fenwray Film Society rules: At a screening on 14 November 2025, a visitor makes a serious threat against another attendee. The visitor has not previously been warned about their behaviour. Must the duty volunteer issue a warning before requiring the visitor to leave?

## Answer

**No. The duty volunteer doesn't have to warn the visitor first.**

Section 2.3 of the Conduct and Complaints rules (`film-conduct.md`) says "Serious threats may result in immediate removal without a warning." Section 1.2 prohibits threats, and section 1.3 requires visitors to follow reasonable instructions from duty volunteers. A serious threat on 14 November 2025 therefore lets the volunteer require the visitor to leave straight away, even though the visitor had no earlier warning.

- **Warning for lesser conduct:** The ask-then-remove sequence in sections 2.1 and 2.2 applies to ordinary disruptive behaviour. Under it, a person who refuses to stop may be required to leave. Section 2.3 is a separate route for serious threats.
- **Discretion:** The rule says "may", so removal is allowed but not compulsory.
- **After removal:** The volunteer must record the incident within 48 hours (2.4). Removal doesn't end the person's membership (2.5), and it doesn't automatically give them a refund right (5.3).
- **Date check:** The changes register (`film-changes.md`) has no amendment to the conduct rules. The version adopted on 18 March 2025 and effective from 1 April 2025 applied on 14 November 2025.
