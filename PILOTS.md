## What the rehearsals found, in numbers

Full folder against documents only, means per session.

| Run | Steps | Text read, characters | Correct, first pass |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rehearsal four | 2.60 against 5.60 | 12,333 against 11,147 | 10 of 10 and 10 of 10 |
| Rehearsal five, first run | 2.60 against 5.40 | 10,083 against 7,591 | 10 of 10 and 10 of 10 |
| Rehearsal five, three runs | 2.50 against 5.37 | 9,985 against 7,536 | 30 of 30 and 30 of 30 |
| Rehearsal five, nineteen arms | 2.70 against 5.40 | 9,753 against 7,359 | 10 of 10 and 10 of 10 |

In every rehearsal the folder took fewer steps on 9 or 10 of 10 questions, answers tied, and the folder put more text in front of the model. By the verdict rule this entry fixes in advance, that pattern is "did not earn its place". So that is what we expect here.

Those figures were produced under the old fault rule, which left out a session for anything unusual. Under the rule this entry uses, one session in the three-run rehearsal that was left out would be kept. It is not in either headline arm.

## The size probe, in short

A library four times the size did not help the folder. Steps did not move in either arm. The control read about 1,600 more characters. The folder's loaded text more than doubled, because its map has a line for every document and is loaded on every question. We expected the opposite and were wrong.

## What a wrong map did

In the nineteen-arm run, a folder whose map described each document with another document's entry took 3.40 steps per session against 2.70 for the true folder: more on 7 of 10 questions, fewer on 2. It still reached 10 of 10 right answers. Seven of ten is not a clear difference under this entry's own rule.
