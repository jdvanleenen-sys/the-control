> Under the Fenwray Film Society rules: At a screening on 14 November 2025, a visitor makes a serious threat against another attendee. The visitor has not previously been warned about their behaviour. Must the duty volunteer issue a warning before requiring the visitor to leave?

## Answer

I don’t have the Fenwray Film Society rules, so I can’t say what they require. I don’t recognise them as a document I know, and I’d rather not guess at a rule that could affect a real decision.

The answer turns on how the conduct or removal provision is worded. Many codes of conduct take a graduated approach (warning first, removal if the behaviour continues) but carve out an exception for serious misconduct such as threats, violence, or harassment, where the person can be removed immediately. Others say “may” ask someone to leave, which gives discretion, while some say “must” warn first, which would make a warning mandatory regardless of severity. Whether a threat counts as “serious” under the rules, or whether the duty volunteer has authority to remove someone on the spot or only the committee does, would also matter.

The 14 November 2025 date may matter too if the rules were amended around then, so it’s worth checking you have the version in force on that date.
