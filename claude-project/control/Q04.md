## Prompt

> Under the Fenwray Film Society rules: A member aged 35 has an active annual membership but is suspended by the committee from 1 November through 20 November 2025. They arrive at a public screening on 8 November 2025 and ask to pay the usual member admission price. Are they entitled to the discount, and what admission price applies instead?

## Answer

I don’t have the Fenwray Film Society’s rules, so I can’t say what they actually provide. If you paste the relevant clauses (membership benefits, suspension, and admission pricing), I can work through the answer precisely.

In the meantime, here is how this kind of question usually resolves, which you can check against the text:

Entitlement to the discount: Member discounts are usually a benefit of good standing, not just of holding a membership. If the rules say suspension suspends membership rights or privileges, a member suspended from 1 to 20 November would not be entitled to the member price on 8 November, even though the annual membership is still active. If the rules instead limit suspension to something narrower, such as voting or committee participation, the discount might survive. The wording of the suspension clause decides this.
Which price applies instead: This depends on how the rules define the non-member tiers. Typically it would be the standard public or non-member price. Since the person is 35, any concession categories (student, under-25, senior) wouldn’t apply on age grounds, though a concession based on something else, like unwaged status, might.
Other points to check: Whether the suspension includes an express bar on attending events (in which case the person might not be admitted at all, rather than just paying more), and whether the membership term is extended or refunded for the suspension period.
