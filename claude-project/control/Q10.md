> Under the Quillbrook Village Hall rules: Four trustees want to attend a committee meeting on 15 November 2026, but one proposes joining by video call from home while the other three attend in person. ## Answer

I can’t answer this reliably because I don’t have the Quillbrook Village Hall rules. No document came through in our conversation, and I don’t want to guess at what they say. If you paste the relevant clauses (or attach the governing document), I can give you a definite answer.

Two things in the rules decide this:

The quorum number for this meeting. Check whether it comes from the governing document itself or from the committee’s terms of reference, since committees often have their own quorum. If the quorum is three or fewer, the three trustees attending in person make the meeting quorate whatever the rules say about remote attendance. If it is four, or a fraction that works out to four, the remote trustee’s status matters.
Whether the rules permit remote participation and count remote attendees. Look for wording on meetings held “by telephone, video conference or other electronic means”. Governing documents that allow this usually count a remote participant towards quorum, but often on conditions, such as everyone being able to hear and speak to each other in real time. Some older or simpler documents say nothing about it, or require trustees to be physically present, in which case the remote trustee would not count.

Check also whether the rules say anything about who may attend or vote at a committee meeting, as opposed to a full trustee meeting.

If you share the quorum and meetings clauses, I can tell you whether the 15 November 2026 meeting would be quorate with three in the room and one on video.
